YEAR 2042 · MARS EXPLORATION

Two rovers. One mission. One shared program.

Mission ARES sent two compact autonomous rovers, ARES-1 and ARES-2, designed to dock together and assemble a larger, more capable exploration vehicle.

During atmospheric entry, a navigation malfunction separated their landing modules. Both landed safely at different, unknown positions. Their parachutes remain on the surface as detectable navigation landmarks.

Communication delays make direct control from Earth impractical. Mission Control can upload one identical CPU program to both rovers, which must then operate independently.

Your engineering mission

Write the shared autonomous rendezvous program that gets the rovers to meet and dock, regardless of their initial landing positions. The simulation models travel along a one-dimensional integer track; it is a programming abstraction of the Martian terrain.

  • Each rover can move left or right along a one-dimensional integer track.
  • Neither rover knows its absolute position or the other's position.
  • The rovers cannot communicate with one another.
  • Each can sense a parachute only at its current location.
  • Both execute the same four-instruction program, one instruction per clock cycle.

Mission success: Both rovers meet and initiate docking, forming the larger ARES exploration rover.

03 / Reference & guidance

Student help

Each instruction consumes exactly one clock cycle, including branches and sensor checks.

left

Move the executing rover one integer position to the left (−1).

Example: 4 → 3

right

Move the executing rover one integer position to the right (+1).

Example: 4 → 5

skipNext

If a parachute is on the rover's current cell, skip the next instruction. Otherwise, execute it normally on the following tick. Senses either parachute, including its own.

On parachute: PC ← PC + 2; else PC ← PC + 1

goto label

Jump to a named label. No movement occurs during this tick.

goto start

label: instruction

Names an instruction as a jump target. Labels and comments are free; instructions cost CPU cycles.

loop: left

Allowed comments: # or //. Blank lines and label-only lines are supported. Commands are case-insensitive. No other commands are permitted.

The task is to produce a single program that works regardless of which rover starts on the left and how far apart they land.

Initial conditions

Two rovers land at distinct integer coordinates. Each parachute stays at its owner's original coordinate for the entire simulation.

Identical CPUs

Both execute the same compiled program, with separate program counters, synchronously. Every tick, both execute their next instruction based on the positions at the beginning of the tick.

Victory

They occupy the same coordinate or pass each other on the same edge during a tick (a continuous-motion encounter). Both halt when they meet.

Run limit

A run can time out; this does not establish impossibility. A program that falls off its end halts that rover and is flagged in the trace.

The coordinate display follows the rovers and parachutes automatically. The line is logically unbounded; the canvas is only a viewport.

Hints are progressively revealed. Try exploring the CPU timing first.

1. If both rovers move in lockstep forever, their initial distance never changes. Think about what information breaks that symmetry.
2. A rover can detect a parachute it did not leave. Could that distinguish the one that started behind from the one in front?
3. All four instructions take a cycle. You can create different average movement speeds by spending some cycles on sensing or jumping rather than movement.
4. Consider a slow patrol loop. When a rover reaches the other landing point, its sensor can route it into a faster loop moving in the same direction.
